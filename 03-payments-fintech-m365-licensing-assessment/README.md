## Engagement Context

A payments/fintech client required a focused assessment of its Microsoft 365 licensing position — specifically, whether purchased entitlements under its Volume Licensing agreement were properly reconciled against actual tenant-level license assignments, and whether the tenant carried avoidable cost or compliance exposure.

## Scope

The assessment was deliberately scoped to Microsoft 365 licensing only — deliberately excluding on-premises infrastructure, server workloads, endpoint configuration, security tooling configuration, and non-Microsoft SaaS subscriptions — to keep the engagement sharply focused on a single, high-value question: **is the tenant's license position compliant, financially optimized, and operationally governed?**

Within that scope, the assessment covered:

- Validation of Microsoft 365 license entitlements under the current agreement structure
- Reconciliation of purchased licenses against actual tenant-level assignments
- Identification of under-licensing and over-licensing exposure
- Analysis of inactive and stale licensed accounts
- Review of service-plan enablement across assigned SKUs
- Financial impact modeling based on observed consumption patterns

## Methodology

Data was drawn from structured exports of the Microsoft 365 tenant environment and the client's official agreement entitlement file — principally a tenant license details export (SKU-level assignment overview, determining assigned vs. available licenses) and a licensed-users report (user-level license mapping, surfacing duplication and over-provisioning).

The analysis proceeded in three stages:

1. **Entitlement vs. consumption reconciliation** — comparing what was purchased against what was actually assigned and in active use within the tenant.
2. **Compliance and efficiency review** — assessing compliance position, utilization/efficiency patterns, and structural/financial risk considerations arising from the license mix (including SKU-tier appropriateness).
3. **Financial impact modeling** — translating the reconciliation findings into a quantified view of cost-recovery and optimization opportunity.

## Categories of Findings

The engagement surfaced the categories of gap typical of a maturing M365 tenant that has not yet implemented periodic reconciliation: over-provisioning at the SKU tier, inactive/stale licensed accounts, and licensing-tier misalignment relative to actual usage patterns. These were assessed as posing moderate compliance risk exposure rather than systemic non-compliance, alongside a clear, addressable financial efficiency opportunity. (Specific figures and the client's consolidated risk position are part of the confidential deliverable and are not reproduced here.)

## Recommendations & Roadmap

Recommendations were structured around: immediate license reallocation and clean-up of stale/inactive accounts, right-sizing of over-provisioned SKU tiers (including evaluating tier "true-down" opportunities where appropriate), and the establishment of a formal, ongoing license-governance framework to prevent recurrence of drift between entitlement and consumption.

## Value Delivered

- A clear, reconciled picture of licensing compliance ahead of any vendor review
- Identified cost-recovery opportunity through removal of stale licenses and SKU right-sizing
- Improved renewal negotiation position through an accurate, evidence-based consumption baseline
- A governance framework recommendation to sustain cost control and compliance readiness going forward, rather than a one-time fix
