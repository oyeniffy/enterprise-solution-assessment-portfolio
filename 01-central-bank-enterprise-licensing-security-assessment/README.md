## Engagement Context

A national central bank operating a large, heterogeneous Microsoft estate — spanning Windows Server infrastructure, SQL Server database platforms, on-premises Exchange Server messaging, and Microsoft 365 user licensing — required an independent, evidence-based baseline of its licensing compliance position, platform lifecycle exposure, and operational risk posture. As is typical of environments of this scale and age, the estate had evolved over time into a complex landscape with limited consolidated visibility across deployments, entitlements, and lifecycle status.

Three categories of driver made this assessment a priority for the client:

- **Licensing and contractual drivers** — the need to validate that deployed infrastructure aligned with Microsoft Volume Licensing entitlements, given that misalignment carries audit, true-up, and contractual-compliance exposure.
- **Lifecycle and supportability drivers** — the operational and security risk introduced by platforms operating beyond mainstream or extended vendor support (absent security patching, elevated breach exposure, reduced vendor escalation options).
- **Operational and governance drivers** — limited centralized visibility into servers, databases, messaging systems, devices, and license consumption, which weakens governance and complicates modernization planning.

## Assessment Objectives

The engagement was scoped around four objective pillars:

1. **Licensing compliance and transparency** — validating Volume Licensing entitlements against discovered deployments across server, database, and messaging platforms, and assessing Microsoft 365 licensing from an entitlement/consumption perspective.
2. **Lifecycle and end-of-support identification** — identifying platforms and database instances operating on unsupported or soon-to-expire vendor lifecycles, and quantifying the operational and security implications of continued use.
3. **Risk identification across infrastructure and licensing** — surfacing risk arising from legacy platforms, infrastructure sprawl, and governance/visibility gaps.
4. **Remediation and optimization roadmap definition** — translating findings into a prioritized, time-phased action plan spanning immediate compliance correction through to strategic modernization.

## Methodology & Framework

The assessment followed a structured, evidence-driven framework designed to keep every finding traceable, defensible, and repeatable — a non-negotiable requirement for a regulator-grade institution.

**1. Automated discovery, not assumption.** Authoritative environment data was extracted using the Microsoft Assessment and Planning (MAP) Toolkit (server/database platform, version, and lifecycle discovery) and ManageEngine (endpoint, device, and inventory discovery), supplemented by usage telemetry and Volume Licensing records. Conclusions were built on verified deployment and consumption data rather than estimation.

**2. Reconciliation-based licensing methodology.** Discovered deployments — servers, database instances, messaging dependencies, users, and devices — were systematically reconciled against available licensing entitlements and usage records, ensuring conclusions reflected actual deployment reality rather than theoretical positions.

**3. Lifecycle baselining.** Security and risk evaluation was anchored to vendor support lifecycle status across the server OS, database, and messaging layers, on the principle that unsupported platforms represent inherent, elevated security and operational risk.

**4. Multi-source validation.** Automated discovery outputs were cross-checked against inventory records and, where ambiguity existed, validated through structured review sessions — ensuring the final baseline reflected the true operational state of the environment rather than raw tool output alone.

## Categories of Findings

Findings were consolidated into a structured gap analysis spanning: discovery coverage and data-quality observations, platform lifecycle and end-of-support exposure, database licensing exposure, messaging platform dependency risk, and Microsoft 365 assignment/compliance alignment — each rolled up into a consolidated gap-and-risk summary and a prioritized risk register for executive decision-making. ## Recommendations & Remediation Roadmap

Recommendations were structured across a time-phased roadmap — balancing quick-win compliance and risk-mitigation actions against strategic, longer-horizon modernization and governance improvements — so that the client's leadership could sequence decisions by urgency and resource availability rather than face an undifferentiated list of issues.
